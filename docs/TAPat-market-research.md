## Market overview

TAPat’s proposed market is **fuel-subsidy administration for Philippine local government units (LGUs)**, particularly programs serving tricycle drivers. Its central proposition is **fund, refuse, return**: release assistance after a sustained price shift within a verified budget; refuse spending outside the card policy and hold suspicious stations for review; return unused card escrow to the LGU at program end. Beneficiary screening, NFC enrollment, receipts, and audit records support those three decisions. These are the intended product capabilities, not a claim that the full application is implemented. [Repository PRD](./PRD.md)

This is an existing market with direct local precedents. Quezon City reports distributing **12,073 fleet cards** as city fuel subsidies to TODA beneficiaries. Its department page was updated in April 2025; the figure represents reported distribution, not a verified current active-user count. [Quezon City transport department](https://quezoncity.gov.ph/departments/tf-transport-and-traffic-management/)

National infrastructure also exists. LANDBANK describes a Fuel Subsidy System that validates eligibility, records purchases, tracks entitlements, and supports station reimbursement. Its April 2026 launch FAQ covers qualifying diesel-powered jeepneys and UV Express vehicles, so that particular implementation should not be assumed to cover every tricycle subsidy program. [LANDBANK announcement](https://www.landbank.com/news/landbank-powers-digital-processing-of-10liter-fuel-subsidy-for-puvs), [launch FAQ](https://www.landbank.com/storage/uploads/files/library/fuel-subsidy-faqs-englishv/fuel-subsidy-faqs-englishv-2026-04-13-957d6488.pdf)

Commercial fuel cards and humanitarian assistance platforms provide additional alternatives. Together, they establish that beneficiary management, restricted spending, card redemption, and blockchain-assisted aid already exist.

**Market interpretation:** TAPat’s likely buyer is an LGU, government agency, or program funder. Drivers receive benefits, while administrators and station personnel operate the system. A plausible entry point is one LGU’s tricycle fuel-assistance program. This is an assessment of product fit, not evidence of procurement demand or willingness to pay.

## Where TAPat could compete

TAPat’s strongest proposed position is **software for administering an LGU fuel-subsidy program with explicit funding, refusal, and return rules**: beneficiary evidence → verified budget → price-triggered allocation → policy-controlled authorization → station review → settlement → unused-balance return and reconciliation.

The prototype specifies one price-triggered release at most per program, using a simulated price series. It is not yet a continuously operating connection to live DOE prices. The intended agents use name matching and conformal risk control, Bayesian changepoint detection, and MIDAS-F plus explicit station rules; their usefulness in a real program remains to be evaluated. [PRD: F1–F5](./PRD.md), [algorithm specifications](./algorithms.md)

Its opportunity is to make these steps easier to administer together. Blockchain, NFC cards, spending limits, and anomaly detection are not individually sufficient differentiation.

A pilot should test:

- Whether administrators spend less time reviewing beneficiaries and reconciling funds.
- Whether duplicate enrollment decreases without wrongly excluding eligible drivers.
- Whether stations receive predictable reimbursement and can resolve disputes.
- Whether the system remains usable under actual connectivity and staffing conditions.
- Whether the total operating cost is acceptable to an LGU.

Existing banks and fuel-card providers could be integration partners as well as alternatives. Their station acceptance and settlement arrangements may be more valuable than replacing their payment infrastructure.

## TAPat’s own gaps and claim boundaries

These limits matter when comparing TAPat with operational programs:

| Area | Current boundary and market implication |
|---|---|
| **Funding and settlement** | Velo verifies the program budget and is intended to sponsor contract calls. It does not verify every fuel purchase. A successful Noir authorization creates a pending merchant entitlement; a later claim transfers tokens. Do not describe a tap as instant merchant settlement. |
| **Return of funds** | Only unused card escrow can return to the LGU. Already-authorized pending payments cannot be clawed back, including those held at a frozen station. “Return” does not mean recovery of misused or already-owed money. |
| **Station restrictions** | Freezing is an application-level refusal and settlement hold. Noir has no merchant allowlist in this design; not every restriction is enforced on-chain. |
| **Identity and physical delivery** | NFC UIDs are clonable identifiers. Enrollment and transaction records do not independently prove who presented a card or that fuel was delivered. |
| **AI and data** | OCR, photo, and OR/CR outputs are precomputed fixtures; calibration and price/activity streams are synthetic. “Ghost-free” and guaranteed fraud reduction are not demonstrated outcomes. |
| **Operational readiness** | Core UI/API workflows remain unfinished. The scope excludes real fiat cash-out, production identity/access controls, and multiple simultaneous programs. Server-held signing keys and shared demo authentication are not a completed production custody model. |

Source: [system design: money flow, contract semantics, and security](./system-design.md), [PRD: demo limits](./PRD.md), [MVP scope and exclusions](./build.md).

## Evidence limits

This research establishes related programs and product capabilities, **not a quantified addressable software market**. Subsidy disbursement amounts are not equivalent to software revenue. LGU willingness to pay, procurement feasibility, station onboarding costs, and actual fraud reduction remain unverified.

The repository’s headline problem figures are not adopted as independently verified market sizing here. The PRD’s broad claim that existing aid has “no rules” should also not be generalized: the cited fuel-card products already document spending restrictions.

TAPat has initial implementation foundations and repository-reported testnet integration results, but its complete subsidy workflow and proposed algorithms are not established as production capabilities. This reassessment read source and documentation without running tests, starting services, or submitting transactions. A competitor’s undocumented feature is not automatically a gap, and a proposed TAPat feature is not automatically a competitive advantage.
